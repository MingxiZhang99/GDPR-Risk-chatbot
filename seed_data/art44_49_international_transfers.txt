Articles 44-49 - Transfers of personal data to third countries

Article 44 - General principle for transfers:
Any transfer of personal data to a third country or international organisation shall only take place if the controller and processor comply with the conditions laid down in Chapter V of GDPR.

Article 45 - Adequacy decisions:
A transfer may take place where the European Commission has decided that the third country ensures an adequate level of protection. Countries with adequacy decisions include: Japan, UK, South Korea, Canada (commercial), among others. The United States does NOT have a general adequacy decision (Privacy Shield was invalidated by Schrems II ruling in 2020). The EU-US Data Privacy Framework was adopted in 2023 but only covers certified US organizations.

Article 46 - Appropriate safeguards:
In the absence of an adequacy decision, transfers may take place subject to appropriate safeguards including:
(a) Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) adopted by the Commission;
(b) Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs);
(c) Approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms.

Article 47 - Binding Corporate Rules:
BCRs must be approved by the competent supervisory authority and must include: application of general data protection principles, rights of data subjects, accepted liability, and how information is provided to data subjects.

Article 49 - Derogations:
In the absence of adequacy decisions or appropriate safeguards, transfers may only take place if:
(a) the data subject has explicitly consented after being informed of risks;
(b) the transfer is necessary for a contract;
(c) the transfer is necessary for important reasons of public interest;
(d) the transfer is necessary for legal claims;
(e) the transfer is necessary to protect vital interests.
