GDPR and Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning - Guidance

1. Legal basis for AI training data:
- Using personal data to train AI models requires a valid legal basis under Article 6
- Consent must be specific to the AI/ML purpose — general consent for "service improvement" is insufficient
- Legitimate interest may apply but requires a balancing test (DPIA recommended)

2. Transparency requirements for AI:
- Article 13(2)(f) requires informing data subjects about the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling
- Meaningful information about the logic involved must be provided
- The significance and envisaged consequences must be communicated

3. Data minimisation in AI:
- Only data necessary for the specific AI purpose should be collected
- Consider whether the AI objective can be achieved with less data or anonymised data
- Synthetic data or federated learning may reduce GDPR compliance burden

4. Rights of data subjects in AI context:
- Right to explanation: data subjects can request meaningful information about automated decisions
- Right to human intervention: Article 22 requires the ability to obtain human review
- Right to contest: data subjects can challenge automated decisions
- Right to erasure: extends to training data and potentially model weights if personal data is memorised

5. Data Protection Impact Assessment for AI:
- Required for large-scale profiling or systematic monitoring (Article 35)
- Should assess: bias and discrimination risks, accuracy of predictions, impact on individuals
- Must document: purpose, necessity, proportionality, risks, and mitigation measures

6. AI-specific risks under GDPR:
- Model memorisation: LLMs may memorise and reproduce personal data from training sets
- Inference: AI may infer sensitive data (health, orientation) from non-sensitive inputs
- Bias: discriminatory outcomes may violate fairness principle (Article 5(1)(a))
- Purpose creep: using data collected for one purpose to train AI for another violates purpose limitation

7. International considerations:
- Training data sourced from EU residents is subject to GDPR regardless of where the AI company is located
- Cloud-based AI services may involve international data transfers (Chapter V applies)
- The EU AI Act (2024) introduces additional requirements that complement GDPR
