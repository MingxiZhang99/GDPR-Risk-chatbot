Article 83 - General conditions for imposing administrative fines

1. Each supervisory authority shall ensure that fines are effective, proportionate and dissuasive.

2. Fines shall be imposed depending on the circumstances of each case, considering:
(a) the nature, gravity and duration of the infringement;
(b) the intentional or negligent character;
(c) actions taken to mitigate damage;
(d) degree of responsibility considering technical and organisational measures;
(e) any previous infringements;
(f) degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority;
(g) categories of personal data affected;
(h) how the infringement became known to the supervisory authority.

3. Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to fines up to EUR 10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher):
- obligations of the controller and processor (Articles 8, 11, 25-39, 42, 43);
- obligations of certification bodies (Articles 42, 43);
- obligations of monitoring bodies (Article 41).

4. Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to fines up to EUR 20,000,000 or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher):
- basic principles for processing including consent (Articles 5, 6, 7, 9);
- data subjects' rights (Articles 12-22);
- transfers to third countries (Articles 44-49);
- non-compliance with an order by the supervisory authority.

5. Notable GDPR fines include:
- Amazon: EUR 746 million (2021) - targeted advertising without consent
- Meta/Facebook: EUR 1.2 billion (2023) - illegal data transfers to US
- Google: EUR 50 million (2019) - lack of transparency and valid consent
- H&M: EUR 35 million (2020) - excessive employee surveillance
- British Airways: EUR 22 million (2020) - data breach affecting 400,000 customers
